How to interpret CS concepts to mom?

Interesting things

During my semesters at UoA, I’ve come across many times when my classmates ask me to explain a concept to them. I found that it’s always a challenge to explain a concept to someone who doesn’t have the same CS background as me. That’s so fun to think about these concepts and their corresponding metaphor.

Fun metaphor

Here’s the list of what I tried to explain those concepts in a fun way. It’s not that absolutely correct, yet it provides a basic view of such concept.

  • Distributed System
    “Lots of commodity computers work together”

  • Consensus Algorithm
    “Multiple nodes agree with one thing, looks as if there’s only one node”

  • Consistency
    “Whether the content is the same on all nodes, right after you write/update the content”

  • Availability
    “No matter which node you ask, someone always answers you, even if the answer might be a little outdated”

  • Partition Tolerance
    “Even if the phone lines between rooms in the house get cut, each room can still keep doing its own thing”

  • Eventual Consistency
    “If you tell a rumor to your friends, they won’t all know it at the exact same instant, but eventually everyone ends up with the same story”

  • Load Balancer
    “A restaurant host who spreads the queue evenly across all the tables so no single waiter gets overwhelmed”

  • Cache
    “Keeping snacks in your pocket instead of walking to the kitchen every single time you’re hungry”

  • Sharding
    “Splitting one giant bookshelf into several smaller ones, each holding only some of the books, so no shelf collapses under the weight”

  • Replication
    “Making photocopies of the same document and keeping them in different drawers, so losing one drawer doesn’t lose the document”

  • Consistent Hashing
    “When you add or remove one drawer from a cabinet, only the stuff near that drawer needs to move, not everything in the whole cabinet”

  • Idempotency
    “Pressing the elevator button five times doesn’t call five elevators, it still just calls one”

  • Message Queue
    “A to-do list stuck on the fridge, one person writes tasks down and someone else picks them up and does them one at a time”

  • Rate Limiting
    “A bouncer at a club who only lets a certain number of people in per minute, no matter how long the line outside gets”

  • CDN
    “Instead of everyone in the world calling the one bakery in Paris for bread, small copies of the bakery open up in every neighborhood”

  • TCP
    “Sending a letter where the mailman makes you say ‘got it’ for every page, and resends any page that goes missing, so the whole letter always arrives complete and in order”

  • UDP
    “Shouting across the room, it’s fast, but if someone didn’t hear you, nobody resends it”

  • DNS
    “A phonebook that turns a person’s name into their actual phone number, so you don’t have to memorize numbers for everyone you know”

  • HTTP/HTTPS
    “Ordering food through a waiter using a shared language everyone agrees on, HTTPS is the same thing but you whisper it in a language only the waiter and you understand”

  • API
    “A restaurant menu, you don’t need to know how the kitchen cooks the dish, you just order by name and get the result”

  • Cookie / Session
    “A wristband from an amusement park, once you get one, the staff at every ride already knows who you are without asking again”

  • Operating System
    “The building manager of an apartment, it decides who gets the elevator next, who gets water pressure, and makes sure tenants don’t walk into each other’s rooms”

  • Process vs Thread
    “A process is a whole kitchen, threads are the cooks in it, cooks share the same pots and ingredients, but two different kitchens share nothing”

  • Multithreading / Concurrency
    “Several cooks working in the same kitchen at once, faster, but they need to take turns with the one stove so they don’t collide”

  • Deadlock
    “Two cooks, each holding one chopstick, both waiting for the other to hand over theirs, and dinner never gets made”

  • Memory: Stack vs Heap
    “The stack is a stack of plates on the counter, you can only add or remove from the top; the heap is more like a warehouse, you can grab or drop things anywhere, but you have to remember where you put them”

  • Garbage Collection
    “A cleaning crew that walks around the warehouse and throws out anything nobody is using anymore, so you don’t have to clean up after yourself”

  • Compiler vs Interpreter
    “A compiler translates the whole book into another language before anyone reads it; an interpreter stands next to the reader and translates it sentence by sentence as they go”
