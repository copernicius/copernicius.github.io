Phone Detox Experiment

Before the experiment 🧠

Actually, getting lost scrolling through my phone has been a common thing for many of us, especially in a modern world, where we are always connected with others through social media. High tech IT companies hire top talents to design delicated algorithms, striving to stick users to their product, and then make profits. Don't be guilty for that, it's not our fault. That being said, we still ought to view this matter objectively. Internet grant us access to abundant information, yet we should not get trapped by cognitive overload. So, I decided to try a phone detox experiment, help me discover a world that I could talk more with myself.

Experiment design

As usual, here’s the 7-day plan of “Phone Detox Experiment”. I made this plan with ChatGPT based on my own experience. Hope I will follow the plan strictly🤣.

Daily note 🤔

Day 1

Target: Awareness - How often I reach for devices?

The day has just begun, I’m hoping to get off to great start💪. We’ll wait and see

  • 8:30 – I woke up, and started my day. The first thing I did was to check my phone for new notifications from Gmail and Wechat. Then sitting on the toilet, I scrolled through Reddit threads about new AI workflow and car modification stuff…
  • 11:30 – Shortly after I wrote down the plan, I felt the urge to scroll TikTok to check if I’d gotten any likes👍🏽. Bloody hell…
  • 14:00 – Went to the toilet, scolling through RedNote for what’s new around me, like car exhaust clips, new harness usage, and stock market exploding news.
  • 17:00 – Working out, frequently checked my phone during set break, even there weren’t any new messages. It’s like a built-in background check, yep, this isn’t the thing I expected.
  • 19:00 – Listening to English podcast when cooking, thinking that I shouldn’t waste any piece of time…
  • 23:30 – Went to bed, and listened to some audio fiction books to help fell asleep, for 30min…

Wrap up: I felt like I was good enough to keep me away from the phone, but actually I was just get lost depending on information from my phone constantly. Maybe it’s because of information anxiety, which means my inner mind is to to as much information as I could get, otherwise I would fall behind. Hah, typical Chinese guy🐼.

Day 2

Target: Embrace of BOREDOM

Today, I wouldn’t use phone during waiting time and toilet time. In my opinion, if I could make it then I am a total “badass🏎️”

  • 8:00 – Got up, going directly to wash and brush teeth. Then cook breakfast without even thinking of the phone, although sometime I could hear a voice from my built-in “bad” system told me to check my phone for something INTERESTING (actuall 99.99% useless content).

  • 9:00 – Went to school, and started my day without taking phone with me. In fact, I felt kind of frustrated that I couldn’t get some “happniess” from time to time.

  • 9:30 – It feels just like quitting alcohol. Even though, I’ve never gone through it myself, my brain was constantly itching to open all unread messages.

  • 12:30 – I did 10min meditation, enduring the “tedious” moment, trying to feel the temperature of my breathe, as well as the noise of the silence. After that, I felt way easier to start working on my school tasks.

  • 14:00 – Headed to the toilet WITHOUT phone. I bet this will be the fastest trip yet🤣.

  • 17:00 – I work out WITHOUT my phone. Thoughts keep popping into my head during rest periods between sets. I wonder if I can bench press 100 kilograms and how I would celebrate hitting that milestone. After all, I’m a total “badass”.

  • 23:00 - I jumped into bed and saw a flood of notifications and messages. Ninety-nine percent of them are just meaningless clutter. This is the honest truth.

Day 3

Target: Focus on depth

Kick short videos and RedNote clips away!

  • 9:00 – I got up and then took a shower with slightly cold water. Unexpectedly, I felt thankful for my plan and began to feel full of energy. I was far more awake than I had been on previous mornings.

  • 10:00 – I read the book Goosebumps, a horror series written for children. It works perfectly for someone like me who does not speak English as a first language. The plot twists are absolutely gripping. Time passed so quickly that two hours slipped away in an instant…

  • 14:00 –I left my phone at home. I brought my laptop to the library and tried writing a blog about what I hae been studying recently. The whole afternoon passed, yet I still haven’t finished it…

  • 22:30 – It was a peaceful night. I lay on my bed and wondered why I could not use the English phrases I had jotted down and reviewed. Could cognitive overload be the reason?

Day 4

Target: Deep work day

Try doing whiteboard recall to check if I have truly mastered a concept

  • 9:00 – Left the phone at home, started my day.

  • 10:00 – Spent all day researching Harness engineering and how we could integrate it into our daily life. It’s so fun. Although I tried to do whiteboard to check my RDMA congestion control protocol knowledge. OK, my bad.

  • 22:30 – Jumped to bed, thought about the research I did today. And by the time, I forget where my phone was.

Day 5

Target: Real life activities

Take part in a volunteer role at Auckland Food Show

  • 8:00 – Got up early, then jogged for 30min at Mission Bay. That great, even though it was cloudy in the morning.

  • 10:00 – Did some household chores, and I was not urgent to touch the phone or trying to check messages.

  • 12:30 – Busy volunteering at the Auckland Food Show, I spoke with attendees and shared the mission of Auckland Mission Grocer with them. Many generous people donated our food packages to those in need. This experience gave me a strong sense of community, and I felt proud to take on this volunteer work.

  • 22:30 – Such a busy day, I had not even 1 minute to scroll through video clips. It was sufficient and fulfilling.

Day 6

Target: Connect with people

Make new friends in the class!

  • 10:00 – I didn’t get up very early because I couldn’t fall asleep last night. Lots of thoughts kept popping into my head.

  • 13:00 – Two stutents were looking for a position in the class, I greeted them and told them to sit down beside me. I started a conversation with them, and we talked about the class and we formed a group to make a presentation. They are from Nepal and Indian respectively. Cuz my listening is not as good as a native speaker, sometimes I struggled to understand them, but finally I nailed by some “Pardon?”. Really, I found the best way of learning is taking action. I became more confident in English, especially when talking with non-native Enlish speaking friends.

  • 17:00 – After some time, I invited them to go fishing this weekend. Yep, more talk, less phone.

Day 7

Target: Reflection

What have I learned from this experiment?

I’d say, I’ve never tried something like this before. I think recording my daily life is a good idea, it doesn’t only help me keep track of what I did and think, but also it’s like a supervisor to monitor and encourage me to continue. It’s much better than just say I would do a “cutting down on phone” experiment.
That said, I don’t totally achieve my goal to get rid of the bad habit, but things are getting better 🤗

What did I get? 🍎

  1. What improved?
    • Feel more self-awareness, sometimes I feel more awake and less anxious.
    • Extended my concentration span, I can continue doing something I rejected to do previously, such as playing “Don’t Starve Together” for 3 hours🤖.
    • Be more patient to people and things around me.
    • It’s easier for me to fall asleep. I probably feel more fulfilled, and I finish more tasks each day than I did before.
  2. What was hard?
    • Stop using phone in the toilet
    • Prohibit the phone usage in the bedroom before going to bed, it’s a habit for many years after all
    • Find a way to stay connected with people in person, I found I am kind of introverted. This is critically important, I must take action to change.
  3. What did I enjoy?
    • Every night I thought I made it~
    • Can think through something that I was not interested in.
    • The experiment was going smoothly, which means it’s well-designed for me.
  4. What will I continue?
    • Insist on carrying out the experiment, build this good habit, and ask my wife to join in.
    • Continue to focus on depth, to feel what’s around me, to enjoy whatever I am doing.
    • Be grateful for every moment, every opportunity, every person in my life.
